Pet resources

When an animal needs help, the next call matters.

Practical guidance for what to do when you find an animal in trouble or need help keeping your own pet.

Emergency or injury

If an animal has life-threatening injuries or severe distress, contact an emergency veterinarian or appropriate local animal-control/public-safety resource immediately.

Stray or abandoned animal

Safely document the location and condition and contact local animal services or a rescue that serves the animal’s location and species.

Need help keeping a pet

Local shelters and pet-resource programs may offer food pantries, low-cost clinics or owner-support programs during temporary hardship.

If you find an injured animal

Keep yourself safe first. Frightened or hurt animals may bite or scratch. If you can do so safely, move the animal away from traffic, keep it warm and quiet, and call an emergency veterinarian or your local animal control or public-safety line right away. Do not give food, water or medication unless a vet tells you to.

If you find a stray or abandoned animal

  • Note exactly where you found it, the time, and its condition. Take a photo.
  • Check for a collar or tag, and ask a vet or shelter to scan for a microchip. Most vets will do this free.
  • Contact the animal services agency for the county where the animal was found. They handle strays and can help reunite lost pets with owners.
  • Post in local lost-and-found pet groups in case someone is searching for it.

If you find kittens outside

Young kittens are often not abandoned. Their mother may be out looking for food. Watch from a distance for a few hours before moving them, unless they are in immediate danger, visibly sick or injured, or in bad weather. If the mother does not return, contact a local shelter or rescue for guidance on care.

Community cats

Outdoor cat colonies are best managed through trap-neuter-return: cats are humanely trapped, spayed or neutered, vaccinated and returned to their colony, where caretakers keep feeding them. Over time this stops the colony from growing. Many counties and local groups offer low-cost spay/neuter for community cats.

If you are struggling to keep your pet

Before giving up a pet, ask your local shelter about pet food pantries, low-cost veterinary clinics and short-term owner support programs. Many areas offer help through temporary hardship so pets can stay with the families who love them.

A note about our rescue

We are a small local rescue and are not able to take intake, adoption or placement requests online. If you have found an animal in need, please contact your county animal services or a shelter near you. If you would like to support the animals already in our care, you can donate here.

Important: This website does not provide veterinary or emergency medical advice. Always contact a licensed veterinarian about an animal's health.
